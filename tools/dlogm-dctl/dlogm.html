D-Log M → DaVinci Wide Gamut

Finally true
colours.

When I shoot with my Osmo Action 6 and Osmo 360 II, they record in DJI’s D‑Log M. When editing, though, I want to work in DaVinci Wide Gamut, because it is larger than the usual Rec.709 gamut. That keeps colours natural, and highlights and shadows keep their information.
However, there is no official description of D‑Log M, only a LUT that converts straight to Rec.709, which throws away a lot of the image information. So far, people in DaVinci Resolve get by with an approximation via a “Color Space Transform”, but that does not match the D‑Log M curve exactly. So I simply measured the curve myself.

The result is a DCTL with a measured curve and colour matrix for both cameras. The DCTL is tested in detail, as described below.

Guide

Grading in three steps.

Step 1 · Install

In Project Settings → Color Management → Lookup Tables, click Open LUT Folder and copy DLogM_to_DWG.dctl into it. Then click Update Lists.

Step 2 · First node

Set the project to DaVinci YRGB and the timeline to DaVinci WG/Intermediate. In the first node of every clip, add the DCTL effect with DLogM_to_DWG.

Step 3 · Grade and output

Now you can simply grade your clips. At the end of every node tree, add a CST to Rec.709. For the DCTL, choose a custom DaVinci tone mapping with a max input of 300 nits.

Background

Why not just a CST?

DJI never published a curve or gamut for D‑Log M. Guides therefore often suggest one of these two options:

  • CST with “DJI D‑Log” as input gamma. This is the obvious choice, and it is wrong. D‑Log M is not D‑Log and not a “true” log profile. The image turns contrasty and oversaturated.
  • CST with “Rec.709” as input gamma. The result looks close to the DJI LUT but squashes the highlights: near the top, one real stop becomes half a stop (compared to the LUT). Exposure changes feel wrong, and skies blow out quickly.

I measured with my ColorChecker Passport:

  • Exposure series in daylight and under a lamp, with fixed ISO and white balance.
  • Clouds changed the light slightly along the way. I could remove that because the Osmo Action 6 stores its own exposure-meter reading in every frame.
  • The Osmo 360 II got its own series under constant light, because its sensor computes exposure differently.
  • The colour matrix of the Osmo Action 6 was calculated from the official DJI LUT. I then aligned the Osmo 360 II to the Action 6.
Tests

DCTL vs. CST.

All comparisons are screenshots from DaVinci Resolve Studio 21. All versions go through the same output transform.

Test 1

Does one stop stay one stop?

The same grey patches shot across four stops of shutter speed, then compensated with an exposure correction. With the DCTL the shots line up to within 0.06 stops. The CST with Rec.709 is off by 0.39 stops, and the CST with “DJI D‑Log” by more than a full stop!

On the Osmo 360 II the DCTL deviates by only 0.03 stops.

Test 2

Grey wedge

Mid grey was neutralised with the Offset colour wheel, then every grey step was measured. With the DCTL the RGB channel values stay together from white to black. With the CST (Rec.709) the dark patches drift apart and the brightest end up at 255.

Action 6, 1/1600 s. Mid grey neutralised with Offset in both. The overlays are Resolve pixel readouts.

Test 3

High dynamic range and contrast edges

This is where the DCTL shows its strength: while the CST method blows out strong contrast edges, the DCTL resolves them better without the whites burning out. Branches against the sky look less “HDR-like”, and clouds show more definition.

Action 6, 1/1250 s. −1 stop is set with the exposure control of the HDR wheel. A small part of the sky is clipped in camera; with the DCTL it turns evenly grey at −1 EV.

Test 4

Saturation vs. brightness

Saturated yellow with a sun reflection is usually a sensor’s nightmare. To make the effect more visible, I deliberately overexposed here and darkened by −1 EV later. With the DCTL the reflection keeps its gradation, even if the bin is slightly less saturated. But saturation is easier to add later than blown highlights are to remove.

Action 6, 1/1250 s. The yellow does not shift hue in either version. The DCTL frame at 0 EV shows a Resolve pixel readout (R 255 / G 211 / B 69).

Test 5

And the DJI LUT?

The official LUT is the convenient way: drop it on, done. But it cements DJI’s high-contrast “look”. With the DCTL the result looks almost the same, but you keep the freedom to change contrast and saturation. The CST method, on the other hand, looks brighter and harsher, and the brightest patches merge towards white.

Action 6, 1/800 s clip. DCTL and CST go through the same output CST with DaVinci tone mapping, max input 300 nits.

So the look of the LUT is not an advantage of the LUT. The difference is where you work:

  • You grade in DaVinci Wide Gamut instead of on finished Rec.709 values. The LUT already delivers the end result, and everything after it works on values calculated for Rec.709 display. With DCTL and CST the image stays in the large working colour space while you grade, and the conversion for display only happens at the end.
  • Exposure and white balance behave correctly. Because the DCTL hits the curve, one stop in Offset is also one stop (Test 1). On Rec.709 values, every correction changes the look at the same time and hits the limits of black and white sooner.
  • The output stays your decision. You set tone mapping and max input in the output CST yourself and can change them later without touching the grade. With the LUT the look is built in.
  • Osmo and Canon end up in the same working colour space. Footage from both cameras can be handled with the same grade and the same output CST.

The CST alone is no substitute. It has the same structure but calculates with the wrong curve and squashes the highlights, hence the brighter, harsher image above. The benefits of the workflow only exist with a conversion that is right from the start. If you just need a finished Rec.709 result quickly, the LUT serves you well.

Test 6

Comparing the two cameras

The 360 II has more highlight headroom than the Action 6, so it got its own curve. Switch with the “Kamera” drop-down menu.

The Osmo 360 II also has its own colour matrix, which maps its colours onto those of the Action 6.

Osmo 360 II, same clip. With its own curve, sky and clouds sit higher while midtones stay the same.

Limits

  • There are no reference values for the colour chart, so there is no absolute ΔE measurement.
  • After alignment, the 360 has slightly stronger greens and reds.
  • What is clipped in camera cannot be recovered by any conversion.
Requirements
DaVinci Resolve Studio DJI Osmo Action 6 / Osmo 360 II D‑Log M, 10‑bit Tested: Resolve 21 · macOS

DCTLs do not run in the free version of DaVinci Resolve. Other D‑Log M cameras were not measured.
