Trail Stability

For a steady run.

My partner shoots her trail-run videos on her iPhone in Action Mode. I usually edit them later in DaVinci Resolve and regularly run into the limits of its stabilizer.

To largely eliminate the oscillation of running in these videos, I built Trail Stability, an OpenFX plugin for DaVinci Resolve Studio.

Star on GitHub

Do we need another stabilizer?

Action Mode already removes the high-frequency shake (from impacts, for example). What remains is a slow sway in the rhythm of the steps: at a cadence of 120 that is 1 Hz at 1 to 2° per step. 2D stabilizers like the one in Resolve misread this in three ways:

Problem 1

The horizon tilts

When your eyes are on the horizon, it stays level as you turn your head. In trail running, though, the head points at the ground. A head turn then (especially with wide-angle lenses) tilts the horizon. Resolve's models read this as a roll movement and “correct” it.

Problem 2

The ground pulls the image forward

Resolve's Perspective model reads the ground rushing past as camera movement and tries to compensate. Distant mountains barely move, though, so compensating pitch and keystone distortions get added and the image rocks like a seesaw.

Problem 3

Heavy zoom

When I stabilize a trail-running clip with Resolve, the clip is zoomed by a factor of about 2. That cuts away much of the frame and also magnifies the remaining shake and image noise.

Trail Stability therefore assumes a 3D camera that only rotates and whose field of view is known. This fills a gap that Resolve itself leaves. Its built-in stabilizer offers three modes:

  • Translation stabilizes through 2D shift only.
  • Similarity stabilizes through 2D shift and rotation.
  • Perspective stabilizes through 2D shift, rotation and tilt.

A method for pure rotation stabilization is missing from this list!

My plugin estimates the true rotation per frame, finds the horizon and computes the smoothest path.
Large, slow movements are therefore preserved. The result is an authentic trail-running clip.

How it works.

1 · Tracking

Track features

Corner points on a 960 px (long edge) grayscale image, pyramidal Lucas-Kanade with a forward-backward check.

2 · Rotation

Estimate 3D rotation

Robust per-frame rotation (2-point RANSAC with reweighting). Chains over 10 frames correct slow drift.

3 · Gravity

Find down

A Kalman filter, forward and backward, estimates the direction of down. This lets the horizon be aligned even when looking at the ground.

4 · Camera path

Solve the smoothest path

A quadratic program (OSQP) finds the smoothest rotation path in which every corner of the zoomed crop stays inside the original frame.

5 · Render

One warp per frame

One homography per frame, bicubic, on Resolve's Metal queue (with CPU fallback).

Numbers beat words.

So that I am not just claiming this is a better version, I measured the “restlessness” of three stabilized trail-run clips by tracking the stabilized result again. The metric is the mean image motion in the central third of the frame.

As mentioned, part of Resolve's higher value comes from the stronger zoom (about 2× versus 1.25×). With other Resolve settings it can do better, but then the stabilizing effect is only present in traces.

What pure rotation cannot remove is the up and down of the body while running. It shows mostly when the ground is very close and is the largest remaining share. I find that authentic for trail runs, though.

Limitations

  • Tuned to the field of view of the iPhone 14 Pro in Action Mode (focal length about 1.02× image width, focalNorm in core/analysis.h). For other cameras this value has to be adjusted.
  • There is one camera path per effect, so the controls cannot be keyframed.
  • Resolve passes the whole source clip to the plugin, not the cut on the timeline. To analyze only short sections of long clips, limit the analysis with the Start and End buttons.
  • Resolve does not respond during the analysis, and it cannot be cancelled.

The protocol and developer options (Analysis Width, Precise Solver, Delete Analysis) are in the README on GitHub.

Requirements
Mac with Apple Silicon DaVinci Resolve Studio Xcode Command Line Tools CMake

I built and tested with a MacBook Pro M1, DaVinci Resolve Studio 21.1.2 and footage from the iPhone 14 Pro in Action Mode. Other setups are untested and unsupported. To prepare for building: xcode-select --install and brew install cmake ffmpeg.
